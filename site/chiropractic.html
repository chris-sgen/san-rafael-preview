What chiropractic care actually does after an injury, and what it does not do

A plain English explanation of the examination and the care that follows, written so that somebody in pain and unsure about money can decide whether this is the right kind of clinic before spending anything or booking anything.

The explanation nobody local actually gives

Most people who have been hurt do one of two things. They do nothing and hope it settles down, or they take painkillers and wait. Part of the reason is that almost every chiropractic page they open is written in practitioner vocabulary they have no way to evaluate. If you cannot tell whether a claim is true, the safest response is to ignore it, and that is what most readers sensibly do.

The version of this page that stood here did exactly that. It was built around a contested technical term and an argument about the body's inbuilt capacity to recover, which is practitioner framing rather than information a patient can act on. It has been rewritten from nothing.

Here is the replacement, in one paragraph. The problem chiropractic care addresses is mechanical: a joint and the soft tissue around it that has become restricted and painful after a force went through it, usually because of how the joint was loaded rather than because of anything wrong with the rest of you. That is the whole claim. Any clinical term that earns its place on this page is defined in the same sentence it appears in.

What you get from care here

Pain with a mechanical cause gets addressed at the cause.

You can get moving again without adding a long-term prescription to the picture. It means the injury is being treated rather than only the sensation it produces.

Movement is measured rather than guessed at.

You can know what your range of motion was on the first day and what it is six visits later. It means progress is a number you can look at rather than a feeling you have to argue about with somebody.

Findings are written down as they are taken.

You have a record of the injury that exists whether or not a claim is ever filed. It means nobody reconstructs your condition from memory months afterwards. Where a personal injury claim is involved, what that record can be turned into is set out on the narrative report page.

What actually happens, step by step

01

History and mechanism.

What we ask about the injury and why it matters. The direction and size of the force changes which structures are likely to be involved, and therefore what gets examined.

02

Orthopedic and neurological examination.

Tests of movement, strength, reflex and sensation. We examine these areas carefully to reach an accurate diagnosis, and everything measured is written down with the date it was measured.

03

Adjustment and manual care.

An adjustment is a controlled, directed movement applied to a specific joint, and that is the entire meaning of the word as it is used here. Manual soft tissue work usually goes with it.

04

Re-examination.

The same tests, repeated. Care is re-tested rather than assumed, and if the measurements are not moving, that is a finding rather than a reason to book more visits.

Some injuries need imaging before care starts. What that involves and what it costs is set out in full on your first visit.

What this page stops claiming

The old version of this page carried a list of claims about what care here does for the rest of your body, well beyond pain and movement. When the practice went through them, the instruction was to drop all of them, and they are gone rather than reworded.

What care can reasonably do.
Chiropractic care may relieve pain and restore movement for many musculoskeletal injuries. That is the claim, stated at its real size.
What it does not do.
It does not treat disease. It has no established effect on the other systems of your body, and this page makes no claim that it does. We do not guarantee an outcome, a recovery time, or a number of visits.

Where medication and surgery sit.

Medication manages pain. Our aim is to address the mechanical injury causing it. Both have a place, and for some injuries you need both. Many patients prefer to try conservative care before considering surgery or long-term medication, and we can tell you whether we think we can help.

When we send you elsewhere.

This is the promise the page is built on. If the examination says this is not the right kind of care for your injury, you will be told, and you will be told what kind of clinician to see instead. That conversation happens after the examination and before any plan of care.

This page is general information, not medical advice. An examination is needed to know whether we can help you.

Questions people ask

Is this wellness care or injury care?

Injury care. This practice is organised around injuries, mostly after collisions and at work. Several practices nearby are built around wellness and family care, which is a different thing rather than a worse one. If ongoing wellness care is what you are looking for, we are not the right fit and we will say so instead of booking you in.

Does it hurt?

The adjustment, meaning that controlled and directed movement applied to one joint, is usually a brief pressure rather than a painful event. It should not hurt. If it does, say so in the room, and the approach is changed rather than pushed through.

How many visits will I need?

No number is promised on this site, because an honest one cannot be given before the examination. What can be promised is that you are re-examined, that you are told what the measurements are doing, and that if they are not improving you will hear that from us rather than have to work it out yourself.

Do I have to keep coming forever?

No. There is no maintenance plan sold on this site and there is no package to sign up to. Care ends when the findings say it should end.

What if chiropractic is not the right care for me?

Then we tell you, and we name the kind of clinician you should see. That is the answer that matters most on this page, because a practice that can only say yes is not much use to you when the correct answer is no.

You get the price in writing before we examine you. The terms and the five ways care after a collision gets funded are set out in full on how care is paid for, and there is no rate card and no per service price anywhere on this site.
