The questions people actually ask after a collision

Some of these answers are "it depends", and one or two are "we cannot tell you that". Those are in here as well.

The rule on this page is simple: we will not tell you what your insurer is going to do, and we will tell you exactly what this practice charges you and when. Everything below follows from that.

Money and coverage

Money and coverage

Will my auto insurance pay for this? Auto insurance often covers care after a collision.

Coverage depends on your policy and the circumstances of the crash. We will check your benefits and tell you what applies to you before treatment starts. What nobody at this practice will do is tell you in advance what your carrier is going to decide, because we do not know.

What if the other driver has no insurance? Then the rail that matters is your own uninsured or underinsured motorist coverage, and in some cases a lien against a claim that is still being worked out.

California is an at-fault state with no personal injury protection, so this question has a different answer here than it would in other states. It has its own page: what happens when the other driver is uninsured.

What does the first appointment cost me? $0 out of pocket to start.

Care here is provided on a lien. You are billed nothing at any point while your case is open, and this practice is paid out of the settlement when the case resolves. If we do not accept your case, if the case is lost, or if it settles for less than the bill, the balance is written off in full and you owe us nothing. Before the examination you are given a written Good Faith Estimate, so you see the numbers that apply to you before anyone examines you. See the written estimate.

What if my claim fails, or settles for less than the bill? You owe us nothing.

That is the whole answer and it does not have a second half. Care is on a lien, nothing is billed to you while the case is open, and if the case is lost, is not accepted, or settles for less than the bill, the balance is written off in full.

Is there a price list? No.

There is no rate card on this site and no per service price, because a published figure would not be the figure that applies to your case. What you get instead is your own estimate, in writing, before the examination. The full mechanism is on how care is paid for.

Timing, and the gap in your record

I felt fine at the scene and was sore two days later. Is that normal?

Yes.

Delayed onset is the rule in soft tissue and whiplash injury rather than the exception. The adrenaline goes, the inflammation develops, and the neck, upper back, shoulder or head starts to complain twelve to seventy-two hours after the collision.

Does it matter how long I leave it before being seen?

It matters to the record more than it matters to the pain.

A period between the collision and the first examination is a hole in the medical record, and a hole in the record is what an insurer points at to argue the injury was not serious or was not caused by the crash. That is a description of how claim files get read. It is not a prediction about yours, and getting examined early does not guarantee anything about a claim. The argument in full is on what to do after a crash.

How soon can I be seen?

New auto accident patients are seen the same day.

That commitment covers new auto accident patients only. The practice's published opening hours are Tuesday to Friday, 9am to 6pm, and for anything other than a new auto accident, request a consultation and you will be told the next available appointment.

The visit itself

What actually happens at the first appointment?

An intake and history, an orthopedic and neurological examination, range of motion testing, and a conversation about what was found.

Diagnostic X-ray is available on site if it is indicated. Chiropractic adjustment, meaning a controlled and directed movement applied to a specific joint, follows only if the examination supports it.

Step by step: your first visit.

Do I have to have X-rays, and what do they cost?

X-rays are recommended in some cases and not in others.

If they are recommended for you, you are told what the imaging is being used to rule in or rule out, you are told the exact cost before anything is taken and it appears on your written estimate, you can decline, and lead shielding is provided.

That block is set out in full on your first visit, and no image is ever taken on the assumption that you will accept the charge afterwards.

What should I bring?

Insurance details, the police report or exchange of information slip, your attorney's contact details if you have one, and a list of your medications.

The intake paperwork is online at the forms page.

Will you adjust me on the first visit?

Only if the examination says it is appropriate.

It is discussed with you before anything is done.

Attorneys, liens and your records

  1. No
    Do I need a lawyer to be treated here?

    Not in most cases, not usually, no. Representation is not a condition of care, and plenty of people arrive here having spoken to nobody at all.

  2. Yes
    Will you work with my attorney?

    On your signed authorisation, examination findings, treatment records and billing go to the firm directly, so you are not the courier between two offices.

  3. Yes
    Will I get a report for my claim?

    A narrative report of your care can be prepared for a personal injury claim. What it contains and how long it takes are set out on the narrative report page, which is the one place either is stated.

  4. No
    Can you tell me what my case is worth?

    This is a chiropractic practice and not a law firm, nothing here is legal advice, and this site does not discuss settlement values in any form. Your attorney answers that question. What we can do is examine you, treat you if we can help, and document what we find.

What is a medical lien?

It is a written agreement that this practice will be paid out of your personal injury settlement rather than out of your pocket as you go.

While the case is open you are billed nothing. When the case resolves, the bill is paid from the recovery. If the case is not accepted, is lost, or settles for less than the bill, the balance is written off in full and you owe us nothing.

The mechanism is set out on how care is paid for.
