The appointment, step by step

The appointment, step by step

  1. 01
    Before you arrive.

    The intake and injury forms are online, so you can fill them in at home rather than on a clipboard in a waiting room.

    They are on the forms page. Bring your insurance details, the police report or the exchange of information slip if you have one, your attorney's contact details if you have engaged one, and a list of every medication you take.

  2. 02
    The history.

    How the collision happened: direction of impact, seat position, headrest, seatbelt, airbag, what hurt at the scene, and what hurt the next morning.

    "I felt fine at the scene" is normal and is not a reason to skip the appointment. The mechanism of the force changes what gets examined, which is why the questions are as specific as they are.

  3. 03
    The examination.

    Orthopedic and neurological testing, range of motion, and palpation.

    What is being recorded are objective findings, meaning measurements and test results that another clinician, or an insurance adjuster, can read and understand later without having been in the room.

  4. 04
    Imaging, if it is indicated.

    Whether X-rays are recommended is decided here, and it is discussed with you out loud before anything is taken.

    The full disclosure is the next section, and it is deliberately not summarised in this one.

  5. 05
    What you are told before you leave.

    Whether this is something conservative care can help, and what a course of care would look like if it is.

    If it is not, Dr. Joseph will say so and refer you to the kind of clinician who can, rather than starting a plan.

  6. 06
    Whether you are treated at the first appointment.

    That depends on what the examination finds.

    If the findings support starting care, it is discussed with you before anything is done, and you decide. We do not publish a blanket answer to this because there is not an honest one.

  7. 07
    How long it takes.

    We do not publish an appointment length either, for the same reason: it varies with what the examination finds, and a figure on a web page that the next patient through the door disproves is worse than no figure.

    Ask when you request the consultation and you will be given the current answer.

X-rays: recommended when, optional always, priced before, shielded always

This section exists because it is the thing this practice has most often been criticised for in public, and the criticism was about disclosure rather than about imaging.

Whether.

X-rays are recommended in some cases and not in others.

If they are recommended for you, you are told what the imaging is being used to rule in or rule out for your specific injury, in plain words, before anything happens.

What they cost.

You are told the exact cost of any X-ray before it is taken, and it appears on your written estimate.

No image is taken on the assumption that you will accept the charge afterwards.

That they are optional.

You can decline.

If you decline, we will tell you what that means for the examination and for your record, and then we will carry on with the rest of the appointment.

That shielding is provided.

Lead shielding is provided as a matter of practice, without you having to ask for it.

What is billed, who it goes to, and what happens if the claim fails

The old version of this page, and the site around it, carried promises about what your insurance would pay and what a visit would cost. When the practice went through those claims, the instruction was to drop all of them. What follows is only what we can tell you about ourselves.

A written Good Faith Estimate is offered before we examine you.

That is not a courtesy, it is a promise this practice publishes and intends to keep. See the written estimate.

$0 out of pocket to start.

Care here is provided on a lien. You are billed nothing at any point while your case is open, and this practice is paid out of the settlement when the case resolves. If we do not accept your case, if the case is lost, or if it settles for less than the bill, the balance is written off in full and you owe us nothing.

When payment is due.

At the resolution of the case, out of the settlement. Not at the desk, not monthly, and not while the case is open.

If we cannot take your case.

Some cases are not accepted, and you are told at the start rather than after a course of care. Where the case is not accepted, the balance is written off in full and you owe us nothing.

Nothing here is given away, and that is the honest version.

Every service is billed: the examination, any imaging, and any care that follows. What the lien changes is who the bill goes to and when. It goes onto the claim, not to you, and it is settled at the end out of the recovery rather than out of your bank account in the meantime. That is why the wording on this site is "$0 out of pocket to start" and never a giveaway offer. The practice has been unclear about this in public before, and this paragraph is the correction.

The five funding rails are MedPay, the at-fault driver's liability cover, your own uninsured or underinsured motorist coverage, a lien against the settlement, and self-pay. They are resolved in full on how care is paid for, and they are not re-narrated here.

There is no rate card and no per service price anywhere on this site. The only price that matters to you is the one written on your own estimate, and you get it before the examination rather than after it.
