What care costs after a car accident in San Rafael, and who pays for it

There are five ways care after a collision gets paid for: medical payments coverage on your own policy, the at-fault driver's liability coverage, your own uninsured or underinsured motorist coverage, a lien against your settlement, and paying for it yourself. This page works through all five and tells you what each one means for you. Before we examine you, you can have the expected cost of your care in writing.

Most people do not know the answer to question one. That is normal, and it is one of the first things we check.

Rail one: MedPay, on your own auto policy

Medical payments coverage, usually written MedPay, sits on your own auto policy. In California it is optional, so you have it only if you bought it. Plenty of people have it without knowing.

Where it exists, it generally responds regardless of who caused the crash, and it is usually a fixed limit rather than a share of the bill. That makes it the simplest money on the board: it does not wait for anyone to admit fault.

We verify the benefit before treatment starts and tell you in plain words what it covers for you. What we will not do is predict what your carrier decides. Coverage depends on your policy and on the circumstances of the crash.

Check your coverage

Rail two: the at-fault driver's liability coverage

The most expensive misunderstanding after a crash is that the other driver's insurer pays as you go. It generally does not. Liability coverage pays at the end, as part of a settlement, once the claim resolves. That is the whole reason the funding question exists, and it is why the other four rails matter.

It is also why waiting is the costly choice. Postponing care until the claim resolves leaves the injury undocumented, and the record of an injury is made by treating it. The interval between the collision and the first examination is called a treatment gap, and it is later read as an argument that the injury was not serious.

We make no prediction about what a claim is worth or how long it takes. Neither should anyone else.

Rail three: your own uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage

Uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage, often written UM/UIM, also sits on your own auto policy. It is designed to respond when the driver who hit you had no insurance, or had a policy too small to cover the injury.

California is an at-fault state with no personal injury protection. There is no automatic pot of money for medical care here. That is why this coverage decides real cases in Marin and why it has its own page.

Whether you carry it, and what it does in your case, is a question for your carrier or your attorney. We describe how the coverage type works. We do not read your policy for you and we do not advise on coverage.

Rail four: a lien against your settlement

This is the one people have heard of and nobody explains. Here it is, in our words, about this practice.

A lien means we treat you now and are paid later out of your settlement, under a written agreement you sign. Your attorney is normally part of that agreement. Accepting a case on a lien is a decision we make case by case, and we tell you yes or no before treatment starts, not after.

The terms, in full:

  • While your case is open, you are billed nothing.
  • We are paid from the settlement when the case resolves.
  • If we cannot accept the case, if the case is lost, or if it resolves below the bill, you owe nothing. The full terms are set out under your price, in writing, before the examination.

All three of those situations end the same way. There is no asterisk on it, and no version of this where an unpaid bill comes back to you.

Two things a lien is not. It is not a discount, and it is not a giveaway: the care is billed, and what the lien changes is who the bill goes to and when. And it says nothing about what your attorney is owed, which is a matter between you and your firm.

Rail five: paying for it yourself

We publish no rate card, and the reason is worth stating plainly. What a collision case costs depends on what the examination finds. A menu price would be a guess, and a guess printed on a website is worse than no number at all, because you would plan around it.

What replaces it is a written estimate for your case, prepared before the examination and handed to you to read. That is a real number about you, not an average about somebody else.

What is billed as its own item

Nothing on this list arrives as a surprise. Each item is priced for you in writing before it is provided.

What we will not promise

  • We do not guarantee that any insurer will pay or cover anything. Coverage depends on the policy and the circumstances of the crash.
  • We do not promise a settlement, a settlement size, or a date.

Questions about money

I do not have an attorney. Will you still see me?

Yes. Having a lawyer is not a condition of care. We do not recommend firms, and questions about your claim belong with an attorney rather than with us.

What if the claim fails, or resolves below the bill?

You owe nothing. That answer is the same whether we could not accept the case, the case is lost, or the settlement comes in under the bill.

Can you tell me what it will cost before you examine me?

Yes, in writing. That is the point of the whole page. See the written estimate.

The driver who hit me had no insurance.

Then the coverage on your own policy is where to look first. See when the other driver is uninsured.

Should I wait until my claim settles to start treatment?

The claim and the injury run on different clocks. A claim can take months. The medical record of your injury is made in the first days by examining and documenting it, and an untreated interval is later read as an argument that you were not badly hurt. Getting examined does not commit you to a course of care. See what to do after a crash.
