Hurt on the job in Marin: how the injury gets examined, documented and paid for

Two questions bring people to this page, usually in this order: who is expected to pay for this, and what is going to be written down about it. Both are answered before you agree to anything, and you get the price in writing before you are examined.

The two ways a work injury goes wrong

The first is the one that happens to the worker. The default after a job injury is to say nothing and hope it settles down, to keep working through it, or to wait until the claim is sorted out before seeing anybody. All three end in the same place. An injury that is never examined is an injury that was never recorded, and a claim file is largely a record of what was written down at the time. Nobody can reconstruct a range of motion six months later from memory.

The second is the one that happened on our side of the desk, and this page exists because of it. What stood here before was syndicated vendor copy: the same sentences a different practice publishes under a different name in a different city. It said nothing about who pays. It said nothing about what gets documented. Nothing on it was specific to this practice or to anybody who walked through this door. That page is gone rather than edited.

What you get here

You are examined properly, not triaged over a phone.

You get an orthopedic and neurological examination of the actual complaint. It means what is wrong with you is established by testing rather than by a form somebody fills in on your behalf.

What is found is written down the day it is found.

You leave with a record that exists independently of how the claim goes. It means nobody has to reconstruct your condition from memory later, including you.

The money question is answered before care starts.

You are told what is billed, to whom, and what happens to the balance if the claim is not accepted, before you agree to anything. It means no surprise invoice arriving after the fact, which is the failure this whole page is written to prevent.

What we actually do with a work injury

01

History and mechanism of injury.

Lifting, repetitive load, a fall, a struck-by. The mechanism changes what gets examined, and it is the thing a claim file most often lacks, because the person taking the report was not asking clinical questions.

02

Orthopedic and neurological examination.

Movement, strength, reflex and sensation, tested and measured rather than estimated. Every term used in the room is explained in the room.

03

Treatment and re-examination.

Manual care, then re-testing. Findings are re-measured rather than assumed, and if the measurements are not moving, that is a finding in itself and you will be told so.

04

The written record.

What was tested, what was measured, what was found, and the date. Released to whoever you authorise in writing, and to nobody you do not.

If imaging is part of the picture, what that involves and what it costs is set out in full on your first visit.

This page is general information, not medical advice. An examination is needed to know whether we can help you.

Who pays, and how that differs from a car crash

A work injury has a different payer from a car crash. A workers' compensation claim is billed to the employer's compensation carrier or to its claims administrator. It does not go to MedPay, it does not go to the at-fault driver's liability cover, and it does not go to your own uninsured motorist coverage. Those are auto rails. If you came here from the money page looking for your answer, that is why it was not there.

What we bill, and to whom. This is settled with you before care starts, in writing, and it is specific to your claim rather than to a general policy. What is billed, who it goes to, when payment is due, and who carries the balance if the claim is denied are all stated to you at the outset. We do not publish a general answer to that on this page, because the honest answer varies by carrier and by claim, and a general answer that turns out not to apply to you would be worse than no answer.

If the claim is denied or disputed.

You are told what that means for your balance before care begins, not afterwards. Nothing starts until you know.

If you were driving for work when it happened.

Both a compensation claim and an auto claim can be in play, and the two are handled differently. The auto side is set out on auto accident injury treatment and how care is paid for.

What we promise either way.

A written estimate of your costs before you are examined. That is a statutory obligation this practice already publishes, turned into a plain promise, and it holds whoever ends up paying.

On your rights under a compensation claim, your employer's obligations, which physician you may treat with, or a medical provider network, this page says nothing at all. Those are questions for your claims administrator or your own attorney, and this is a chiropractic practice rather than a law firm.

Questions people ask

Can I see a chiropractor for a work injury at all?

This practice examines, treats and documents work injuries. Whether your particular claim covers it is a question for your claims administrator or your own attorney, and it is not one we can answer for you.

Who pays for the visit?

Coverage depends on the claim and on the carrier. We will check and tell you what applies to you before treatment starts, and it is put in writing rather than said over a phone. We make no representation about what any carrier will decide.

What if my claim is denied?

You are told what happens to the balance before care starts, so a denial is not the moment you find out. It is part of the written estimate conversation, not a surprise at the end of it.

I was in a crash while working. Which claim is this?

Possibly both, and they are handled differently. The auto side of that answer lives on auto accident injury treatment.

Do I need my employer's approval before I come in?

The rules on this differ by claim, and we cannot advise you on them. Your claims administrator can, and so can your own attorney. What we can tell you is what an examination here involves and what it will cost you, before you commit to anything.
