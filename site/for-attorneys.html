02 / Your firm / What you are checking for

The three things that go wrong with a treating clinic

Records arrive late, or arrive as a billing ledger.

What we produce instead is a narrative report in the format set out on the report page, requested once. You should be able to put a date in your own diary for when it lands.

The findings are subjective and cannot be re-read six months later.

Orthopedic and neurological testing is recorded as measurements, with re-examination, and the reasoning is written in plain clinical language. You can read the file yourself and know what it says without needing the person who wrote it to interpret it.

There is no one to call.

One named doctor, Dr. Michael Perry Joseph, D.C., examines the client, authors the report, and takes the call. A question about the file is a phone call, not a request form.

Each of those is checkable before you send anyone. Call and ask him what the examination covers, and ask what the report contains.

04 / This practice / What travels with the referral

What else comes with the referral

Liens.

The practice accepts medical liens. Your client is billed nothing at any point while the case is open, and the practice is paid from the settlement. If the case is not accepted, is lost, or settles for less than the bill, the balance is written off in full and your client owes nothing. That is the practice's own term, not a reading of it, and it is stated the same way to the patient on the cost page. It says nothing about what your firm is owed, which is a separate matter between you and your client.

A written estimate before examination.

The client is offered a Good Faith Estimate of costs in writing before being examined. For a firm that is the useful part: the treatment cost is a known figure early in the file rather than a surprise at settlement.

Onward referral.

If the examination indicates care this practice does not provide, we say so and say what kind of clinician is needed. We name no outside facility on this site and we do not describe the arrangement as a formal network, because it is not one.

Scheduling.

A new auto-accident patient is seen the same day. That commitment covers a new patient who has been in a collision, and it is the only timing commitment this practice makes.

06 / Both sides / The questions that get asked

The questions firms ask

How do I refer a client?

Call the practice on (415) 877-9400, or use the consultation form and say the referral is from your firm. Tell us the client's name, the date of the collision and what you need from the file. There is no proprietary intake portal to learn.

What is the turnaround, and what happens if it slips?

No figure is published, for the reason in what a firm can verify before sending anyone. Agree the date at referral. If it is going to slip, you will be told before the date rather than after it, and there is no guarantee attached to it that we could not keep.

Will the doctor testify or provide an opinion for the case?

This practice publishes no credential of that kind and makes no claim of that kind. What it produces is the clinical document described above. Anything past that document is a question for you and your client.

Is any of this legal advice? Do you work the claim?

No. Not a medical doctor's office, no surgery, no prescriptions, no legal advice and no representation, and not an emergency room. That is the whole boundary.

Do you refer clients to attorneys, or take referral fees?

No. No referral fees are paid or received, and there is no financial, discount or reciprocal arrangement with any orthopedist, neurologist, imaging facility or physical therapist this practice refers to. Confirmed by the practice owner on 2026-08-19.
