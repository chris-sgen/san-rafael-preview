Accessibility statement

This is the accessibility statement for the website of San Rafael Auto Accident Injury Chiropractic Center, Dr. Michael Perry Joseph, D.C., an auto-injury practice of Pain Relief Clinic of Marin.

What it covers
This website.
What it does not cover
The clinic building itself, which is a separate matter and one we have not published anything about, so please call and ask about the premises rather than infer anything from this page. It also does not cover pages on other websites we link to, or the consultation form, which runs on a system the practice does not control. What has been built into this site says more about that.

If something on this site stopped you from doing what you came to do, telling us about a barrier is the part you want, and it is one phone call.

What has been built into this site

Each of these is a thing you can check, and each one is a change from the site this replaces.

  • Alternative text on every image that carries meaning, and empty alternative text only on images that are decoration. On the site this replaces, nearly every image on the home page carried empty alternative text, whether it was decorative or not.
  • One first-level heading per page, and headings in order below it, so a screen reader's heading list reads as an outline of the page rather than a jumble. The old home page had no first-level heading at all.
  • Every control reachable and operable from the keyboard, with a focus indicator you can see. If you can click it, you can tab to it and use it.
  • Tap targets of at least 44 by 44 pixels, including the small social icons, which measured 40 by 40 on the old site and were the one control that failed that floor there.
  • Text that reflows at 200 percent zoom without pushing the page sideways.
  • Real labels on form fields, not a grey hint inside the box that disappears the moment you type.
  • Colour is never the only thing carrying meaning, and text is checked for contrast against the background it is actually painted on rather than against plain white. The practice's existing brand colours conflicted with each other, and resolving them without breaking contrast was part of the rebuild rather than an afterthought.

One named limitation, in the same section as the list rather than buried below it. The consultation and appointment form is supplied by an outside vendor and runs on their system. The practice cannot warrant that embedded form's accessibility, and until control of it is resolved this statement does not cover it. If the form is the barrier, call instead. The number is under telling us about a barrier, and the practice will take the appointment over the phone.

The standard this site works to, and what has not been verified

We build this site to work toward WCAG 2.1 Level AA.

No accessibility audit has been carried out on this site. So we do not claim conformance with that standard, we do not display a certification badge, and we do not show a third-party seal. None has been earned. If an audit is commissioned later, the level it finds and the date it was done will replace this paragraph, and not before.

This is the same rule the rest of the site runs on. When the practice went through the claims on the old site that could not be traced to something a reader can check, the decision was to drop all of them. A conformance claim nobody has audited is exactly that kind of claim, so there is not one here.

What we know is still outstanding:

  • The third-party consultation form described under what has been built into this site.
  • The intake forms offered as PDF downloads on the forms page. They have not been checked for tagging or reading order, and an untagged PDF is a real barrier. If you need those forms in another format, call and ask, and they will be read to you or sent another way.
  • Assistive-technology testing across specific screen reader and browser combinations. We are not going to list combinations we have not actually tested on.

Telling us about a barrier

Call (415) 877-9400 and say you are calling about the website. That reaches the practice during its published opening hours, which are on the contact page.

What helps us fix it faster
The page you were on, what you were trying to do, and the assistive technology or browser you were using.
What happens next
A person reads it and replies. We are not publishing a response-time promise, because the practice has not committed to one, and an accessibility promise that gets missed is worse than none at all.

The practice has no published email address at this point. When there is a monitored mailbox it will be added here.
